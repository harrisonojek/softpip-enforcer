Risk of ruin is the mathematical probability that an account will reach zero (or a specified terminal drawdown floor) before hitting its profit target. Most small retail accounts in Nigeria operate with a 100% risk of ruin because their position sizing is misaligned with the statistical distribution of losing streaks.

### The mathematics of losing streaks

In an independent trials system where your win rate is `p` and your loss rate is `q = 1 - p`, the probability of encountering a streak of `n` consecutive losses is given by `q^n`.

If you trade 5-tick Rise/Fall contracts with a win rate of 55% (`p = 0.55`, `q = 0.45`):
- The probability of 3 consecutive losses is: `0.45^3 = 0.0911` (approx. 1 in 11 sequences).
- The probability of 5 consecutive losses is: `0.45^5 = 0.0185` (approx. 1 in 54 sequences).
- The probability of 8 consecutive losses is: `0.45^8 = 0.00168` (approx. 1 in 595 sequences).

Over a single session of 20 trades, an 8-trade losing streak appears rare. But when you deploy an automated bot that executes 40 trades per day, you execute 1,200 trades per month. Over 1,200 trials, the probability of experiencing a streak of 8 or more consecutive losses is greater than 86%.

If your position sizing model cannot withstand 10 consecutive losses without liquidating your margin, your account is guaranteed to fail given sufficient sample size.

### The asymmetry of drawdown recovery

The second mathematical reality you must memorize is the non-linear relationship between drawdown and required recovery return:

| Account Drawdown (%) | Capital Remaining on $10 | Gain Required to Break Even |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **10%** | $9.00 | **11.1%** |
| **20%** | $8.00 | **25.0%** |
| **30%** | $7.00 | **42.8%** |
| **50%** | $5.00 | **100.0%** |
| **75%** | $2.50 | **300.0%** |
| **90%** | $1.00 | **900.0%** |

When your $10 account drops by 20% to $8.00, recovering to your initial capital requires a 25% gain. This is achievable within normal operating parameters.

However, once your drawdown crosses 50%, you no longer have a position-sizing problem; you have an insurmountable mathematical obstacle. Sizing up to recover a 50% drawdown requires risking an even greater percentage of remaining equity, triggering the terminal spiral observed in Phase 06 of the autopsy.
