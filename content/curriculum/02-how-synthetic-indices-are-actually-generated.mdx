If you trade Volatility 75, Volatility 100, or Crash/Boom expecting technical analysis to work the same way it does on EUR/USD or crude oil, your account will eventually reach zero. Synthetic indices are mathematical simulations, not traded auction markets. You are not trading against human buyers and sellers whose collective fear and greed form price discovery. You are trading against a deterministic pseudo-random number generator (PRNG) with constant volatility parameters.

### Cryptographic generation mechanics

Deriv synthetic indices are generated by an algorithm that produces a discrete tick every second (or two seconds depending on the asset configuration, such as Volatility 75 1s). The formula follows a discrete stochastic process:

```
S_(t+1) = S_t × (1 + σ · ε_t)
```

Where:
- `S_t` is the current spot price of the synthetic index.
- `σ` is the annualized volatility constant (e.g., 75% for Volatility 75, 100% for Volatility 100).
- `ε_t` is a pseudo-random variable generated cryptographically with a mean of 0 and a variance of 1.

The crucial property of this generator is that `ε_t` is identically and independently distributed (i.i.d.). Each tick has no mathematical relationship to the preceding tick. The probability that the next tick is higher than the current tick is exactly 50% (minus the infinitesimal probability of a dead flat tick).

### Why chart patterns fail on PRNG feeds

In traditional financial markets, a double bottom or head-and-shoulders pattern represents an accumulation or distribution phase driven by institutional order flow and resting limit order liquidity. 

On a synthetic PRNG feed:
1. **Support and resistance are visual illusions**: If you plot random walks on a chart, your brain will identify trends, trendlines, channels, and support levels. This is pareidolia—the psychological tendency to perceive meaningful patterns in random visual noise.
2. **No liquidity pools**: There are no stop-loss orders clustered above swing highs for market makers to sweep. Price does not "bounce" off a moving average because an algorithm has no knowledge of what indicators you have overlaid on your chart.
3. **No mean reversion**: If Volatility 75 drops 1,000 points over ten consecutive candles, the probability of the next tick being green remains exactly 50%. The market is not "oversold" because there is no fundamental equilibrium price.

When you trade synthetic indices, your only manageable variable is position sizing and risk containment. If you rely on technical indicators to predict direction with a supposed 75% certainty, you are building your risk model on a mathematical fallacy.
