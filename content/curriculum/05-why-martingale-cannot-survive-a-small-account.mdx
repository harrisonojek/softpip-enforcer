Martingale is an 18th-century betting strategy in which the stake is doubled after every losing round so that the first winning round recovers all previous losses plus a profit equal to the original stake. On YouTube, Telegram, and retail trading forums, it is routinely sold as an "automated profit system" for binary options and synthetic indices.

In reality, Martingale is the single fastest mechanism for liquidating a small account on Deriv.

### The exponential stake escalation

The mathematical flaw in Martingale is geometric progression. When you double your stake after each loss, your capital requirement scales exponentially:

```
S_n = S_1 × 2^(n-1)
C_n = S_1 × (2^n - 1)
```

Where `S_1` is your initial stake, `n` is the sequence step, and `C_n` is the cumulative capital risked across `n` steps.

Starting with Deriv's minimum contract stake of $0.35:
- Step 1: $0.35 (cumulative: $0.35)
- Step 2: $0.70 (cumulative: $1.05)
- Step 3: $1.40 (cumulative: $2.45)
- Step 4: $2.80 (cumulative: $5.25)
- Step 5: $5.60 (cumulative: $10.85)

Notice the cumulative requirement at Step 5: **$10.85**. 

If your total starting capital is $10.00, you cannot even place the Step 5 contract. You are liquidated at Step 4, having lost $5.25 of your $10.00 account, with only $4.75 remaining in equity. When the algorithm attempts to place the $5.60 order at Step 5, the broker rejects the contract for insufficient margin. Your account has suffered a fatal 52.5% loss in five minutes.
