## The setup

Between January 14, 2025, and October 28, 2025, I lost ₦32,450,000 (≈$21,600) across Deriv synthetic index Options contracts and MT5 CFD positions.
*Converted at ₦1,500 to the dollar.*

The accounts were funded through three local bank wires:
- January 14, 2025: ₦4,500,000 ($2,960) initial principal to Deriv account CR3928104.
- July 16, 2025: ₦11,200,000 ($7,368) reload following an unhedged liquidation event.
- October 22, 2025: ₦16,750,000 ($11,020) final deposit during a 7-day attempt to recover the drawdown before month-end accounting.

I was trading high-volatility synthetic indices: primarily Volatility 75 (1s), Volatility 100, and Jump 50. Synthetic indices run on cryptographic pseudo-random number generators with constant volatility. Unlike foreign exchange or equities, there are no bank holidays, no earnings releases, and no central bank interventions. The price feed does not mean-revert because there is no fundamental asset beneath it.

I treated synthetics as if they possessed structural memory. I assumed that seven consecutive bearish 1-second candles increased the mathematical probability of a bullish tick on candle eight. That assumption was mathematically false, and it formed the foundation of every subsequent error.

## Position sizing

My initial sizing plan in January 2025 specified risking no more than 1.5% of total equity per trade. On an initial capital base of ₦4,500,000, that meant a maximum stake of ₦67,500 ($44) per contract.

For the first eight weeks, I complied with the rule. By March 28, 2025, account equity had reached ₦9,180,000 ($6,040) across 382 logged positions. Win rate sat at 58.4% on 5-tick Rise/Fall contracts.

The breakdown began when I adjusted the sizing model:
1. **Dynamic ratchet without downward adjustment**: When equity reached ₦9.18M, I increased the base stake from $44 to $150. When equity subsequently dropped to ₦7.4M in April, I refused to reduce the stake back to $44, arguing that smaller stakes would delay the recovery of the drawdown.
2. **Capital aggregation**: I maintained trading capital, reserve capital, and operating funds in a single Deriv wallet. The bot and my manual MT5 terminal drew from the same balance pool. A drawdown on manual trades directly impaired the margin headroom of automated scripts.
3. **The 3.5% trap**: On smaller secondary accounts I ran simultaneously, I funded $10 to test strategies. Deriv's minimum contract stake for Volatility 75 Rise/Fall is $0.35. A $0.35 stake on a $10 account is 3.5% of total capital on trade one. At that ratio, a streak of just 18 consecutive losses wipes 63% of the account, rendering recovery statistically improbable without excessive risk.

## The missing stops

On Deriv Options contracts (Rise/Fall, Higher/Lower, Matches/Differs), there is no conventional trailing stop or discretionary mid-trade exit once a tick contract is accepted by the server. Your risk is fixed at the stake amount, but your risk exposure per unit of time is extreme.

On the MT5 CFD side (Volatility 75 and Volatility 100 1s lots), stops were technically available but systematically omitted:
- **Mental stops instead of hard server orders**: Over 84% of manual positions entered between June and October were submitted without a hard `Stop Loss` price. I monitored positions manually on mobile, intending to close when the loss reached $200.
- **Slippage during volatility spikes**: On Volatility 75, a single 1-second price jump can span 45 points. When manual market orders were submitted to exit a losing position, execution slipped by an average of 4.2% beyond the intended exit threshold.
- **Removing stops when tested**: In 27 logged instances, hard stops that were initially placed were manually dragged lower as the price approached the trigger level, converting a planned $180 loss into an eventual $1,400 liquidation.

The absence of hard, automated, non-negotiable stops at the API layer guaranteed that any tail-risk event would consume the entire account rather than a predetermined risk slice.

## The unaudited tooling

In May 2025, I transitioned from manual execution to an automated Node.js WebSocket script connecting to `wss://ws.derivws.com/websockets/v3?app_id=...`.

The script was intended to enforce rules. Instead, it accelerated losses due to four architectural flaws:

1. **Unchecked WebSocket reconnect loops**: When the local internet connection dropped in Warri, the client re-established the socket connection but lost local state. On reconnection, it re-sent the pending order payload without verifying whether the previous order had already been filled on Deriv's server. On July 12, this caused 14 duplicate $200 contracts to execute within 800 milliseconds.
2. **Martingale multiplier hardcoded in memory**: To recover from losing trades, the script implemented a 2.0× stake multiplier on consecutive losses. A sequence of 5 losses escalated the stake from $25 to $50, $100, $200, $400, and $800. The cumulative capital risked across those 6 steps was $1,575.
3. **Lack of account balance validation**: The script calculated stake sizes relative to the initial session balance rather than polling live equity before dispatching a proposal. When equity fell below the required margin, Deriv rejected contracts, but the script entered a spin loop that exhausted API rate limits and blocked emergency manual intervention.
4. **No local kill switch**: There was no external heartbeat monitor, no balance floor check, and no daily loss cap. The script continued firing until the Deriv API returned error code `InsufficientBalance`.

The tool was never backtested across 10,000 ticks. It was tested across 40 ticks on a Saturday afternoon, declared functional, and connected to live capital on Monday morning.

- **Physical degradation**: Chronic sleep deprivation, insomnia from overnight positions running on VPS, high resting heart rate, and severe focus deficit.

- **Psychological distortion**: When you trade without a risk governor, you lose the ability to evaluate risk objectively in daily life. You treat ₦500,000 as "just five contracts" instead of recognizing it as several months of living expenses.

The experience permanently destroyed any belief in trading intuition, chart patterns on synthetic RNG feeds, or recovery ladders that double stakes after losses.

## What changed

SoftPip exists because I refused to hide the loss or pretend it was bad luck. It was not bad luck. It was structural incompetence combined with unaudited tooling and emotional revenge trading.

To prevent this failure pattern from ever repeating, I built **The Enforcer** and wrote the ten-module curriculum around four non-negotiable engineering principles:

1. **Hard daily loss cap ($1.00 on $10)**: The risk governor process terminates execution immediately when the cumulative session loss reaches $1.00. No manual override is permitted until the 00:00 UTC rollover.
2. **Elimination of 2× Martingale**: The Enforcer caps any recovery ladder at 1.3× base stake, strictly limited to 5 steps ($0.35 → $0.46 → $0.59 → $0.77 → $1.00). If step five fails, the ladder resets to $0.35 flat. Total risk across all five steps is $3.17, preserving 68% of a $10 account.
3. **Mandatory 15-minute cool-off**: Following two consecutive losses ($0.81 cumulative loss, safely below the $1.00 daily loss floor), the bot pauses all order dispatch for 900 seconds. This physically removes the trader from the market during choppy regime shifts before the daily loss cap can terminate the session.
4. **Account segregation**: Trading bots run on a dedicated Deriv sub-account with no more than $10 funded. Discretionary trading on the bot account is prohibited.

If you are trading on Deriv without hard limits, you are one bad evening away from repeating this sequence.
